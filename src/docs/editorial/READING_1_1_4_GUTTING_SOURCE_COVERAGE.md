## Argument as presented by Gutting

Gutting moves from Sartre's intentional, non-substantial consciousness to negation, radical freedom, bad faith and authenticity. He then explains the poststructuralist reaction. Foucault works **centrifugally** into histories of ideas, knowledge and social practices; Derrida works **centripetally** back into the texts and problems of philosophy. These are distinct projects, not a demonstration that one philosopher logically refutes the next.

Crucial qualifications retained in the visible reading: Sartre's freedom determines the **meaning of events**, not the physical events; the psychological self is an **object** of consciousness; atheism is largely a **starting point** rather than a completed proof; Foucault's power and knowledge are **interrelated, not identical**, and his account allows objective or perhaps universal truths; Gutting's **three principles** of logocentrism are his organizing labels rather than a formula Derrida states; and Derrida's ethical leap returns to the task of constructing **new rational law**, not abandoning rationality.
