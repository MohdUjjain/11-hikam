# Study Aid Roles and Reading Design Protocol

**Project:** 11AM — Takhassus Hikam Dossier

**Adopted:** 28 November 2026

**Status:** Binding editorial instructions for the whole existing and future corpus. Adoption of these instructions does not mean the existing reader has already been migrated.

**Scope:** Arabic and English main notes, study aids, orientation, optional reading assistance and independent revision, subject to the layer-specific rules below.

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.** Preserve the supplied source's meaning, attribution, reasoning and degree of certainty. No instruction to clarify, condense, classify, visualize or aid recall permits new intellectual claims, evidence, objections, conclusions or invented hedging.

Read this document with the [language and source-fidelity protocol](LANGUAGE_AND_SOURCE_FIDELITY_REFINEMENT_PROTOCOL.md), [concise dossier protocol](CONCISE_DOSSIER_AND_READING_COMPANIONS.md), [context-first Companion and Source Depth protocol](CONTEXT_FIRST_COMPANIONS_AND_SOURCE_DEPTH.md), and [design system](../design/DESIGN_SYSTEM.md). ## 1 Authority and precedence

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

The source controls the intellectual content. The repository controls the implementation architecture. The user controls the authorized work and any change to the editorial policy. Apply the latest explicit user decision where discussions contain successive versions of a rule. A re-pasted historical discussion does not, merely by being included in a documentation request, revive a rule that the user explicitly replaced.

This protocol consolidates the November study-aid decisions. It supersedes conflicting older instructions about component roles, limits, main-body examination tips and checkpoints, EL10 placement and the scope of routine verification. The original Premium Study Notes prompt supplies the educational objectives and repertoire; its broad permission to add examples is constrained by the later source-fidelity and EL10 decisions. Its NotebookLM wording does not make generated teaching notes a higher authority than the original reading.

The operative changes include definition limits of 20 words per entry and 40 per two-entry box, one opening central concept, the 30-word memory limit, 10-word normal and 20-word maximum table cells, and 7–15-word roadmap points. Historical 30-word definition rules and statements that these limits are undecided have no current instructional force.

This documentation update authorizes documentation work. It does not itself implement chapter rewrites, a new palette, new component types, typography changes or prose-to-bullet conversion. When implementation is authorized, apply the current rules to the agreed scope and preserve unrelated material. Do not infer permission to rewrite the entire corpus merely because the policy applies throughout it.

## 2 Intellectual fidelity and source preparation

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

Read the complete assigned original, the existing dossier and its revision material before changing its intellectual presentation. Consult the three supplied Total Recalls or other teaching syntheses as comparison aids. Where a corresponding DOCX or native document is supplied, prefer that editable original for reading over PDF extraction; verify doubtful OCR, names, formulas and quotations against the available original image or scan. Never claim to have read missing or inaccessible material.

Maintain a source-unit record covering the thesis, premises, evidence, examples, definitions, distinctions, classifications, qualifications, objections and replies, conclusions and argumentative connections. Record the originating author, source location, degree of certainty and intended visible or optional home. Include footnotes, tables and appendices where they contribute to the assigned reading. A coverage checklist supports close reading; it does not replace it.

Preserve distinctions between the primary author's position, another person's position reported by that author, a quotation, historical report, empirical assertion, hypothesis and conclusion. An attributed claim must not become an unqualified fact through shortening or highlighting. Conversely, a categorical source claim must not acquire assistant-authored doubt. Preserve source-supplied objections and replies without adding others for artificial balance.

Do not silently repair an identifiable substantive error, contradiction or unclear reference by changing the author's intellectual position. Record the specific editorial issue separately when necessary. Any independently requested verification must be distinguishable from source representation. Do not invent citations, quotations, statistics, dates, names, examples said to be from the source or apparently precise page references.

Keep the full original readings, private language samples and restricted source files outside a public repository unless their publication is explicitly authorized. Permission to use them for notes is not permission to upload them. Repository editorial records may contain necessary bibliographic metadata, source locations, coverage decisions and links without reproducing the source collection.

## 3 Educational purpose and learning priority

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

Every page should improve comprehension, navigation and retention. Comprehension means understanding the source's ideas and reasoning. Navigation means finding material and recognizing its place in the discussion. Retention means recovering what has already been understood. A component earns its place through an identifiable learning operation, rather than its colour, name or presence in a template.

Separate three questions: **role** identifies what the component does; **priority** identifies how necessary the material is to this reading; **provenance** identifies whose statement, example or device it is. None is a substitute for the others.

| Priority | Meaning | Placement requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Essential | Necessary to understand the position, follow the argument or preserve an indispensable distinction or qualification | Available in the principal reading path and ordinary print composition |
| Useful | Improves understanding, orientation or retrieval once the essential account is available | Place at the point where its learning operation helps |
| Supplementary | Further source detail that can be deferred without misrepresenting the essential account | May occupy a justified optional layer |

Use the accepted test: **If the reader skips this material, can they still understand the author’s argument accurately, including its necessary boundaries and qualifications?** If not, the material cannot exist only in a closed disclosure or popup.

Do not assign priority automatically from presentation. A table, definition, quotation or source analogy may be essential. Ordinary prose is not essential merely because it is prose, and a coloured box is not merely useful by definition. These categories do not rank truth, endorse a position or predict examination frequency. The final visual treatment of priority remains a separate design decision; the intellectual distinction applies now.

## 4 Preservation across the reading layers

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

Preserve educational substance and necessary relationships, rather than treating every sentence of a generated explainer as immutable. Improving organization can remove redundant exposition while retaining distinct premises, cases, qualifications and replies. A long explanation must not disappear merely because a concise aid cannot contain it.

| Layer | Responsibility | Counting and access |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main explanation | Teach the essential question, reasoning and conclusion in connected scholarly language | Main-prose pool; independently readable and printable |
| Main study aids and objectives | Define, distinguish, structure, clarify, orient or support recall | Main-aid pool, with the applicable individual limits |
| Frontmatter and roadmap | Identify and orient the reading | Separate from the main-body planning pools |
| Reading Companions | Resolve an immediate difficulty in the existing passage | Optional screen assistance; counted separately |
| Source Depth | Preserve further, genuinely optional source detail | Separate, normally closed and excluded from standard print |
| Independent revision | Review and retrieve material already taught | Separate from main-prose and main-aid pools |

The established programme guidance remains approximately **650–850 main-prose words plus 850–1,150 main-aid words**, scaled to the assigned source's length and complexity. Main aids include section objectives. Exclude frontmatter, roadmaps, Companions, Source Depth and the independent revision block from those two pools. Ordinary body lists remain prose unless they actually form an approved study component; list markup alone does not change ownership.

The programme ranges are source-proportionate planning guidance, not an instruction to fill boxes or delete indispensable reasoning. Record material departures and their source reason. Individual hard limits still apply: necessary longer exposition must receive a suitable accurate form and location. Source Depth has no overall word quota, but its optional-source function is binding; it is not an overflow route for essential material.

## 5 Counting units and the current numerical rules

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

Count the complete visible body of a bounded aid. Paragraphs, list items, inline contrasts, formula explanations and multiple subparts share its allowance. More paragraphs do not create more allowances. The definition family has the explicit additional per-entry and entry-count rules below.

| Component or unit | Current numerical rule |
| --- | --- |
| Opening central concept | Exactly one per dossier; 40–75 body words |
| Individual definition | At most 20 words and two sentences |
| Definition box | At most two definitions and 40 body words; definitions only |
| High-yield note | At most 40 body words |
| Common mistake | At most 35 body words |
| Memory aid | At most 30 body words |
| Compiler-added analogy / EL10 / ELI10 | 20–30 body words; one explanatory comparison within the aid |
| Source quotation | No new word maximum |
| Author-supplied analogy / simple explanation | No new numerical maximum; source reasoning and attribution govern |
| Authored table cell | Normally 10 words or fewer; 11–20 only when accurate meaning requires; never above 20 |
| Complete roadmap point | 7–15 words, including its bold topic lead-in |
| Hierarchy, map, timeline and objective | No additional numerical maximum selected; their functional concision rules apply |

Use the established bilingual convention: whitespace-separated tokens containing letters or numbers count as words. Attached Arabic particles remain attached; hyphenated words remain one token. Decorative icons and standalone punctuation or arrows do not create words. Preserve the visible integrity of formulas, exponents and symbols when extracting text. Do not concatenate separate table cells or list entries, and do not insert artificial spaces into a continuous mathematical expression merely to change a count.

For aid-body limits, exclude the category label, actual title, decorative icon and separately presented quotation attribution. Include meaningful inline terms, the defined term when it is part of the entry, explanatory labels and all substantive body clauses. For roadmap points, the bold topic lead-in is part of the point and counts. A long title or attribution must not become a place to hide substantive exposition excluded from a body count.

Maintain both **body-word** and **full-visible-word** measurements when evaluating the programme budget. State which is reported; never compare one basis with the other as though they were identical. In the audit baseline, full-visible aid totals include their meaningful titles and labels, while body totals isolate the bounded explanation. Optional hidden text, repeated accessibility labels and duplicate Companion anchors must not inflate either visible pool.

Bounded units retain their limits when reused in revision: individual definitions, memory devices and table cells are still bounded. The complete Key Definitions section, revision grid or memory collection does not receive a 20-, 30- or 40-word overall cap. A collection can contain multiple separately identifiable entries; a definition callout may still contain no more than two definitions.

## 6 Central concept or thesis

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

Provide **exactly one central-concept aid at the start of each dossier**, in the opening teaching position after the existing identification/orientation material. Keep it within **40–75 body words**. Do not add another central-concept box at a later section or conclusion. An independent revision summary is a separate revision function, not permission for another main-body concept box.

State the governing question or thesis and the essential relationships needed to recognize the discussion's direction. Preserve a qualification when it belongs to the central position. The box should help a reader place later detail; it should not attempt to contain the full evidence inventory, every subsidiary argument or the whole chapter summary.

Reassign extra concept boxes by actual operation. A comparison may become a table, a sourced rule sequence a logical structure, and a necessary explanatory synthesis ordinary teaching or another approved concise role. Preserve the source substance. Do not relabel everything high-yield or place it in an unapproved general-purpose category.

Source length may inform a **soft drafting target within 40–75**, but no source-page formula creates a new per-dossier maximum. Distinguish main assigned readings from supplementary collections, combine files belonging to one dossier, and do not double-count PDF/DOCX versions. The distribution-based calibration in the audit document is provisional. A source-accurate thesis controls the eventual wording; do not pad it to reach a calculated target.

## 7 Definitions and grouped definition boxes

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

A definition identifies a term's meaning in the source and relevant context. Each entry contains **at most 20 words and two sentences**. A definition box contains **one or two definitions only**, with **40 words as the combined maximum**. Neither the sentence allowance nor another entry's unused words can enlarge an individual definition beyond 20 words.

Group definitions only when their relationship helps study. Two entries are a maximum, not a requirement. A 30-word box containing three definitions fails the entry-count rule; a 40-word box containing a 10-word and a 30-word definition fails the individual rule. A box containing one definition cannot use the two-entry allowance for an additional discussion.

Retain the source's technical meaning and indispensable differentiating conditions. Do not replace a technical concept with a loose everyday synonym. If accurate teaching needs more explanation than the compact definition can carry, place the explanation in the main teaching or another justified approved component and keep the two visibly connected. Do not fabricate a shorter definition by dropping the condition that makes it correct.

Historical comparisons, procedures, objections and replies, illustrations and argument summaries are not definitions merely because they concern unfamiliar ideas. During migration, identify the actual definition units before counting them. Record term, source meaning, entry words, sentences, group size, any mixed material and its proposed source-faithful destination. Existing whole-box counts cannot establish compliance with the new per-definition rule.

The proposed general-purpose explanatory callout remains unresolved. Until its purpose, name and cap are agreed, use an existing suitable role or ordinary explanatory text. Source analogy is not a generic substitute label for all explanation.

## 8 High-yield notes

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

A high-yield note isolates a particular source-grounded point whose understanding or retention matters to the central idea, section reasoning or consequential distinction. Keep the complete body within **40 words**.

Suitable material includes an indispensable distinction, a condition on which the source's argument depends, an explicitly drawn implication, a conclusion connecting previous steps, or a source-supplied qualification easily lost during recall. Its value must be intelligible in its immediate context. Attribution remains clear when the proposition belongs to a particular thinker or is reported rather than endorsed by the primary author.

Do not equate high-yield with likely examination frequency. Do not use it for procedural authoring metadata, a statement about the file's scope, an exercise asking the reader to reconstruct an argument, a long catalogue, or a general claim that the passage is important. A retrieval chain belongs to memory when retrieval is its actual operation. Extended source exposition remains necessary teaching and must receive sufficient space elsewhere.

The 40-word ceiling does not permit a new synthesis beyond the author's conclusion. Preserve numerical and conceptual qualifiers; a compact fact box must not make a source estimate exact or a conditional claim unconditional.

## 9 Common mistakes and textual misinterpretation

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

This aid identifies a specific possible misreading of the supplied material and gives the source-faithful distinction that resolves it. Its complete body has **at most 35 words**. It addresses the idea being taught, without becoming an independent assessment of the author's adequacy.

Legitimate cases include conflating two terms the source distinguishes, attributing a quoted view to the primary author, generalizing beyond the source's stated scope, reversing a dependency, treating an illustration as the conclusion, or confusing the role of a premise and a reply. The source must support the boundary being taught.

Do not add an objection, consensus correction, dismissive label or warning that a source position is unproved, overstated, unacceptable or unsupported merely because the assistant prefers another evaluation. If the primary author supplies an objection or reply, represent it with its actual ownership and certainty. A source's own criticism of another argument is source content; an assistant-created criticism is not authorized by this callout.

If the needed explanation is longer than 35 words, keep the concise misreading distinction here and place necessary reasoning in the normal teaching path. Avoid packing several independent corrections into one box merely because they share a topic.

## 10 Memory aids

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

**A memory aid provides a recognizable cue through which the reader can reconstruct source-grounded items, order, distinctions, relationships or numerical anchors.** The complete cue, key and explanation together have **at most 30 words**. There is no new minimum or per-dossier quota.

| Method | Legitimate retrieval operation | Fidelity requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mnemonic or acronym | Recover a set of terms or stages | Each element maps accurately to taught source material |
| Memorable phrase | Recover a distinction or sequence | Memorability must not alter the proposition |
| Visual association | Attach a retrieval cue to a supplied concept | The cue is identifiable as a teaching device |
| Memory palace | Organize items through imagined locations | The locations are not source facts or logical parentage |
| Conceptual anchor | Reattach details to the source's organizing idea | Do not invent a replacement thesis |
| Comparison trick | Recall the feature separating related concepts | Preserve the source's actual contrast |
| Pattern recognition | Recover a recurring supplied structure | Do not impose a pattern absent from the material |
| Remember this because | Recall why a distinction matters here | The reason must come from the source's discussion |
| Ordered recall chain | Retrieve meaningful stages or dependencies | Arrows and ordering retain their source meaning |
| Numerical anchor | Pair a number with its referent and role | Preserve units, context, attribution and qualification |

These methods expand the available repertoire; they do not require invented acronyms or a memory palace in every dossier. A cue that introduces more arbitrary items than it helps recover is unsuitable. Understanding comes before memorization. The source items should already be taught, and the reader should be able to reconstruct the intended meaning without guessing the encoding.

Check that the heading and body name the same number and order of steps. A heading listing three stages cannot accompany a four-stage chain without correction. Distinguish exact duplication from legitimate complementary retrieval: repeating concepts from a table may be useful if the cue performs a separate recall operation, but copying the table's explanation is not enough.

Compiler-created encoding or phrasing is permitted as a recall device, not as new academic content. If the device explains the concept through a new analogy, place it in the dedicated EL10 role and apply its 20–30-word rule. A memory label must not conceal an added explanatory analogy. An analogy supplied by the author retains its source ownership even when it is memorable.

## 11 Compiler-added analogy and EL10

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

**Analogy / EL10 / ELI10** is one name group for a dedicated compiler-added explanatory analogy. It is not an extra universal technique that may be inserted under any aid label. Use the established analogy presentation, with provenance made clear, for **20–30 body words**. One aid should convey one comparison and its relevant connection; this is not a requirement for one analogy in every dossier or section.

The permitted addition is a simpler comparison that clarifies an existing source idea. It cannot add a premise, empirical evidence, philosophical position, causal mechanism, judgment, objection or conclusion. Identify the source relation to be explained before drafting the comparison, and check that the comparison preserves precisely that relation. Reconnect it to the technical idea so the reader does not retain an unrelated story.

The analogy must not carry the argument's only explanation or become evidence for the source's conclusion. Do not introduce unsourced scientific or historical assertions as the apparently familiar side of the comparison. Do not append invented reservations about the author's argument as an analogy limitation. If the comparison cannot be expressed accurately and usefully within the allowed range, omit that device and teach through a suitable source-based form.

A literal simpler restatement of an existing idea in a Reading Companion does not become EL10 merely because its language is easy. Conversely, a newly invented explanatory comparison cannot be placed inside a Companion, memory box or high-yield note to evade the dedicated-role restriction. The earlier instruction permitting EL10 inside any suitable aid is superseded.

## 12 Author analogies and source quotations

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

An analogy supplied by the author, including one quoted or discussed from another person, belongs to the source's own presentation. The agreed label group **author's analogy / simple explanation** refers to that source-supplied explanatory comparison. Preserve who originated it, who reports or uses it, the point of comparison and any inference the source draws from it. No new numerical cap has been selected for this source material.

Classify by actual function after establishing provenance. An example, metaphor, thought experiment or illustration may serve a source analogy, but a direct conceptual restatement or historical discussion is not automatically an analogy. Knowing that a passage comes from the source does not give every paragraph an unlimited callout allowance. Place direct explanation, definition, classification and comparison according to their own functions.

Unknown provenance is an unresolved editorial question. Do not presume that an existing EL10 label proves compiler authorship, or that familiar wording proves source authorship. Consult the original and record the passage and page. Until resolved, do not represent the material as verified source content or apply the wrong type's limit solely from its legacy class.

A source quotation preserves actual words where wording matters. Keep its attribution, source location, relevant context and degree of certainty. No new quotation word cap is imposed. Do not rewrite a quotation to fit another aid's limit or present a paraphrase as a verbatim quotation. If a quotation is too long for a table cell, place the intact necessary quotation outside the table and use a faithful short reference or paraphrase within the cell.

Preserve distinctions between an original quotation, a supplied translation and an editorial paraphrase. Suspected transcription errors require checking the original; fabricated quotation marks or invented page references are never acceptable. Quotations must not become a device for labelling ordinary assistant explanation as uncapped source text.

## 13 Tables and comparison structures

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

Tables may organize direct comparisons, classifications, catalogues, matrices, terminology, chronological references and scholars' attributed positions. Their value is the ability to compare or retrieve information across stable axes. Two columns are allowed when they suit that operation; a fixed three-column quota would be as inappropriate as putting every explanation into two columns.

Keep each authored instructional cell at **10 words or fewer normally**. Use **11–20 words only when preserving accurate meaning requires them**. **No cell may exceed 20 words.** This applies to body cells, row headings and column headings, including revision tables. Count the complete physical cell once even if it spans several rows or columns; paragraph breaks and merged positions do not multiply its allowance.

Choose comparable row units and meaningful column criteria. A student should be able to follow a column and understand the repeated question it answers. Preserve the subject and attribution of each position. Avoid fragmenting a qualification across distant cells so that the principal claim becomes misleading when read alone.

For an oversized cell, determine whether the material contains several comparison dimensions, separate stages, a definition plus explanation, or necessary prose beside the table. Restructure accordingly without omitting substance. A long original quotation should remain an accurate quotation outside a bounded cell. Do not solve a limit by shrinking the font, removing spaces, hiding text, adding arbitrary columns or replacing meaningful distinctions with cryptic abbreviations.

Use semantic column and row headers where they represent the table's relationships. Preserve a legitimate empty corner header where appropriate; do not insert filler to avoid a zero count. Captions should identify the table's subject or operation without carrying displaced paragraphs. No additional caption-word cap has been selected.

Retain the shared `.tbl-wrap` and `table.comparison` architecture. The observed later row-header styling comes from the existing broad `table.comparison th` selector acting on body-row headers; it is not evidence of a replacement design system. Standardize row-header, column-header and caption treatment in the authorized design pass, preserving semantic markup, Arabic readability, horizontal overflow and print behavior. Do not silently remove row-header semantics to imitate an earlier appearance.

## 14 Hierarchies and logical structures

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

Determine the relationship before choosing numbering or indentation. A list's CSS depth does not prove that the source establishes parentage or dependency.

| Actual relationship | Appropriate treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Parent and subordinate categories | Decimal hierarchy with genuine parent-child nesting |
| Successive argument steps | Ordinary sequential numbering |
| Parallel concepts or alternatives | Equal-level labels or numbers |
| Mixed illustration, premise and conclusion | Identify each role before assigning numbering or nesting |

For a genuine hierarchy, use a consistent progression such as 1, 1.1, 1.2, 2, 2.1. Returning to a higher level returns to that level's numbering. Do not continue a single flat counter through subordinate branches, and do not manufacture empty parent categories to make decimals fit.

An argument's third premise is not automatically a child of its second premise. Several examples can support one proposition without becoming successive proof stages. A conclusion follows from the relevant premises as the source presents them; an arrow or indentation must not strengthen that connection beyond the source.

For mixed structures, first label what is an illustration, condition, premise, objection, reply or conclusion. Retain concise node text and place indispensable extended explanation beside the structure when needed. No additional hierarchy-node word maximum has been selected. Preserve actual relationships rather than shortening every existing node indiscriminately.

## 15 Concept relationship maps

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

A relationship map displays short concept labels and explicit short relations between them. It should let the reader see how the ideas connect. Labels such as depends on, contrasts with, is a type of and leads to express different relationships and must not be treated as interchangeable arrows.

Use source-established relationships and preserve their direction, scope and strength. Do not turn association into causation, an example into a universal case or one author's report into an endorsed conclusion through a diagram. Mixed Arabic/Latin terms, numbers and arrows must retain the intended reading direction.

Do not turn nodes into paragraphs or append a substantial discussion inside the map merely to keep it under the same visual wrapper. Review attached prose by its function: retain a concise relationship label within the map and place necessary explanation in the appropriate adjacent teaching location. Preserve its source content and count it in its actual new layer.

No blanket compression of already short nodes is required, and no new node-count or node-word quota is imposed. The known attached-paragraph cases in 3.1.5 and 3.2.5 require specific review; they do not establish that all current maps are defective.

## 16 Historical timelines

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

Use the established timeline when chronological progression is itself useful: successive events, dates, historical positions or stages genuinely ordered by the source. A conceptual classification does not become chronological merely because its subjects lived at different times. Use a table or logical structure where simultaneity, contrast or conceptual dependency is the actual relation.

Preserve stated dates, approximate dates, uncertainty and gaps. Do not invent years or events to fill a visual sequence. Identify whose position or event is represented and the source-supported relevance of its place in the sequence. Keep event titles and descriptions readable without turning the timeline into a second prose chapter.

No new event-word maximum has been selected. Existing Arabic timeline font, spacing, direction and marker placement require component-specific verification in the authorized design pass. Do not impose arbitrary dates or alter source chronology to solve layout.

## 17 Section objectives and chapter roadmaps

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

A section objective states the actual question or purpose of the section immediately ahead. Keep it concise, intelligible and consistent with what the source discussion establishes. It is orientation, not a hidden checkpoint demanding an answer. No new objective-word limit has been selected; count objectives in the main-aid planning pool.

Each roadmap point contains **7–15 words**, including a **bold topic lead-in followed by a short, source-faithful preview**. The preview says what that part examines, explains or establishes. It should help the reader anticipate the discussion rather than merely reproduce the section heading. The roadmap belongs to frontmatter and is outside the main-aid budget.

A point may group several related sections when its preview accurately covers them. The number of points is not fixed. Do not invent a topic to fill the range, promise an absent argument, strengthen an inquiry into an established conclusion, or pad a short heading with generic language. Preserve the source's progression and certainty when choosing verbs.

Restore meaningful bold lead-ins in the later roadmaps. The whole point, including that lead-in, must fit 7–15 words; the lead-in is not a decorative label excluded from counting. Arabic points need natural previews in Arabic, not literal English templates or a list of copied headings. Review emphasis, line wrapping and RTL list padding together during presentation work.

## 18 Retired aids and function-based reclassification

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

Retire **main-body examination tips and checkpoints** across the corpus when the migration is implemented. This concerns their teaching function as well as the CSS class or title. A guided test, reconstruction exercise or answer-writing prompt must not survive in the main notes merely by being relabelled high-yield, memory or another callout.

Review the content before removing the wrapper. Preserve any unique source explanation or necessary distinction in an appropriate visible teaching location. Appropriate practice may belong in the independent revision questions if it serves that established function. Do not retain every old quiz prompt as prose, and do not delete source reasoning simply because it was placed inside a retired aid.

Classify remaining material by the operation it actually performs. Distinguish a term definition, source example, contrast, argument sequence, retrieval cue, source quotation, ordinary explanation and author-supplied analogy. A container's legacy label does not establish its role or provenance. Role correctness and length compliance require separate checks.

The retirement does not delete technical reading-position checkpoints, legacy identifiers required for saved-state continuity, or immutable archived chapter content. Do not globally remove every occurrence of the word checkpoint from code, history or revision. Preserve the repository's historical snapshots and unrelated functional contracts.

## 19 Complementary teaching and aid distribution

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

Treat the dossier as notes and summary working together. Preserve the central explanation and provide complementary ways to understand and retain difficult material. A definition, table, source example and memory cue can each contribute a different operation. Repeating one explanation in four boxes does not create four complementary operations.

After removing retired components and shortening bounded aids, review each section for missing work: a term needing definition, an actual distinction needing comparison, an argument needing sequence, a relation needing a map, a textual confusion needing correction or a set of items needing retrieval. Add a suitable source-grounded aid where that need exists. A difficult idea may justify a dedicated EL10 analogy within its narrow permission.

Maintain the source-proportionate aid emphasis of the programme. Do not simply reduce the total teaching content and move on. Equally, do not add a fixed number of boxes to recover a numerical deficit, manufacture memory devices or create repetitive small boxes by splitting one long discussion at arbitrary word intervals.

Paragraph presentation is not itself a defect: a short paragraph can contain a meaningful chain, paired distinction, formula or inline sequence. Judge the complete aid's role, source fidelity and length. Ordinary prose converted to complete-sentence bullets remains a separately deferred task; this study-aid migration does not authorize a wholesale rewrite of body presentation.

## 20 Reading Companions and Source Depth

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

Follow the detailed [context-first protocol](CONTEXT_FIRST_COMPANIONS_AND_SOURCE_DEPTH.md). A Companion answers what the current phrase means and why it appears here, optionally with a brief recall handle. Simpler restatement is allowed when it resolves the reader's immediate difficulty; a new fact is not required. Do not introduce an unrelated scholarly detour to justify a popup.

Anchor a Companion to one exact existing main-prose occurrence. Preserve source words, quotation boundaries, copying, search and reading position. Do not attach cues by ambiguous fuzzy matching, inside quotations or by accidentally swallowing neighboring text. Optional clarification must be locally complete, and the surrounding reading must stand without opening it.

Further optional source detail belongs in normally closed, paragraph-adjacent native Source Depth disclosures. Group records concerning one paragraph within one disclosure; keep each record's source ownership and location clear. Source Depth must work without JavaScript and remain outside standard print composition and its measurement clones. It must not produce hidden or unreachable search hits.

Neither optional layer can be the only home of indispensable reasoning, objections, conclusions or qualifications. No planned print appendix cures an incomplete current reading. Source Depth has no overall word limit, but a record still needs a justified source contribution. The new limits do not create a universal popup-word cap; any actual bounded definition, mnemonic or instructional table used inside a layer retains its unit rule.

## 21 Independent revision system

> **This permission does not authorize an independent judgment that the author’s position is unproved, generalized or unacceptable.**

| Revision section | Required operation |

Keep the five question categories in order: **Short Answer, Long Answer, Comparison, Conceptual, Application**. Questions must be answerable from the taught material and preserve the author's framework; do not add an independent philosophical debate or external factual claim under an application label. The original prompt's examination purpose survives here despite retirement of main-body tips and checkpoints.

Check revision after main-text changes so it does not retain old errors, omit restored distinctions or introduce material never taught. A source-critical idea cannot appear for the first time only in revision. Collect actual mnemonics rather than inventing a disconnected second set. Apply the definition-entry, memory-device and table-cell limits to those units without capping entire revision collections.

Key Table is an established heading, not a requirement for exactly one table in every case. Existing return-note sections need precise, useful directions and must serve the revision function; do not silently delete them because their physical format differs. Whether to replace all such return notes with reproduced tables is not a newly adopted rule.

The One-Page Revision Sheet is a rapid-review function. Claim an actual one-page printed result only after checking the relevant print composition. Do not change page geometry, A4 boundaries or the established pagination system merely to make the heading literally true.
